## Tried and rejected (local measurements)

- **Per-thread wall-clock timeline** (step 18, 12 threads, `full`, docker, quiet runs about 47 ms in process): main reaches `fast` at 0.15 ms; worker threads start 0.3 -> 3.0 ms (each spawn about 0.25 ms on this host); the regions barrier ends at about 2.3 ms (regions parse is only about 2.3 ms of single-thread CPU, 1% of the work, so the barrier just waits for the spawn ramp); all threads finish parsing within about 1 ms of each other; the final drain takes under 0.1 ms; output formatting and the write take 0.3 ms; about 3 ms falls outside `main` (exec and exit). The only serial section is the spawn ramp.
- **Tree spawning** (each new thread spawns half of the rest; main runs worker 0): the last thread starts at a median of 2.2 ms instead of 4.4 ms (instrumented, 12 runs each), but wall time shows no difference. Without PGO, paired 200 rounds: faster in 94/200, median +1.0 ms. With a fresh PGO profile: faster in 75/200, median +8 ms. The host was 2x oversubscribed (load 6-9), so the ~1 ms it could save is unresolvable here.
- **Chunk count after step 19** (`--chunks` 192/256/384/768/1536): peak RSS 13.8/13.7/13.9/15.0/16.8 MB median; 384 kept.

- **Record ends from "LF followed by a digit or BOM" with an even quote count** (no PCLMUL in the block scan; per record a 64-byte comma window for the id, a 64-byte quote and comma window with prefix-XOR for the name/note split, and a 64-byte window ending at the terminator whose last two commas start region and amount; exact, fuzzed): correct on every test, but 224.8M vs 202.7M instructions on `sample` (1 thread, cachegrind), +11%. The three per-record windows cost more than the shared 64-byte block scan they replace. Timing (25 rounds, interleaved) no better.
- **No 128-record stage** (aggregate and send in `record()`, no region prefetch): 12 threads min 75.7 vs 74.3 ms, median 94.4 vs 93.5 (25 rounds); 1 thread 316 vs 259 ms. The region-entry prefetch earns its lines.
- **File pages dropped every 64 KiB during the scan** (instead of once per chunk) plus the batch pool in its own mapping: peak RSS unchanged within sampling noise, 12 threads 11% slower (each `MADV_DONTNEED` is a TLB shootdown across all threads of the process).
- **Shared name table with `fetch_or` group bits** (no messages; 478 instructions per record): 20% slower than step 9 at 12 threads, and slower at 4 threads. Lines written by `lock or` on every record bounce between cores.
- **Shared intern table giving dense ids + 4-byte (id, group) messages over SPSC rings to bitmap owners**: 529 instructions per record and the slowest variant (83 ms vs 56 ms at 12 threads, 97 vs 76 at 4). The owner model keeps each slot range in its owner's L2; the shared table does not.
- **Two-pass scan** (all separator positions of a 4 KiB window written with unconditional stores, then 5 per record): 0.9 fewer simulated branch misses per record but more instructions, and slower in every timing session.
- **PREFETCHW** for the name line, **non-temporal stream prefetch**: no gain.
- **Per-record sequential parser** (each field located with a 32- or 64-byte compare from the previous field's end; chunk starts checked by chaining record ends instead of quote parity): parsing 336 -> 295 instructions per record, but 20% slower on 1 thread and no faster on 12. The field-to-field address chain is serial, while the block scan's masks do not depend on the parse position.
- **Prefetch lead across batch boundaries** (one look-ahead cursor over the whole drained chain), **prefetching batch messages before the tag probe**, **`MADV_POPULATE_READ` per chunk** (page faults are only ~4.3k per run), **one SWAR for the whole amount** (-4 instructions per record), **smaller chunks** (raised peak RSS): no gain or within noise.
- **Parse micro-batch after step 15** (`num` without its `e < 8` check, guaranteed by requiring the header's LF at >= 8; one pshufb classify for ',' and LF; a plain shift instead of `low()` in `quotes`). The `num` change saved 7 instructions per record (535 -> 528). The shift saved nothing (the compiler already knew), and the pshufb masks *added* ~300 per record, unexplained. The `num` change was rebuilt with a fresh seed-7 profile and timed against step 15 in docker: 12 threads -5.6% in one session and +15% in the next, 1 thread +4.6% and -1.3%. Host noise exceeds the effect, so it was not landed.
- **Per-thread private distinct sets**: `full` has 2.97M distinct (name, country) pairs in 3.32M records, so nearly every record is a new pair. Per-thread sets would hold ~270k pairs each (far beyond L2), and the merge would redo the dedup.
- Ablation (step 12, 12 threads, laptop, min of 21): scan only 16.5 ms, plus field parsing 30.8, plus aggregates and message build 31.5, plus batch passing 36.7, plus owner table updates 60.3. The table updates are 90 of 509 instructions per record but 40% of the time.
- Ablation (step 9, 4 threads, laptop): parsing only 33 ms, plus message passing 44 ms, plus owner table updates 73 ms. The name table is the memory-bound part.
